# Genuine or Phantom?

*A research draft, November 1, 2026. Follow-up to The Tightness Paradox. Not yet part of the paper. First-pass analysis: genuine_or_phantom_run.py and genuine_or_phantom_supp.py; results in genuine_or_phantom_results.csv, genuine_or_phantom_supp.csv, genuine_or_phantom_states.csv, genuine_or_phantom_perstate_pipe.csv, all in this folder.*

## 1. The question, and why it decides what the main paper means

The Tightness Paradox ends with a located effect and a reading of it. The effect: since 2022, in the third of states where hiring was hardest before COVID, unfilled openings predict higher wages (+0.650, pre-registered equality test p = 0.0099). The reading: labor in those states is scarce enough that a posting which stays open really does bid wages up.

That reading assumes the unfilled postings are real attempts to hire that are failing. There is a second possibility, and it is the one the original class paper started from. A posting can stay unfilled because nobody was ever going to be hired into it. Ghost postings, evergreen requisitions kept open to build a pipeline, postings left up after the search quietly ended. If the openings behind the high-friction states' coefficient are substantially that kind of posting, the scarcity reading fails. The coefficient would be a fact about posted data, not about hiring difficulty, and the paper's headline would narrow to a statement about what employers advertise rather than what they cannot do.

The published ghost-jobs literature cannot settle this. It measures intent and prevalence: Ng, Tong and Abdelwahed classify Glassdoor interview reviews and put the ghost share at up to 21 percent of ads; Greenhouse's platform classification puts 18 to 22 percent of its postings in the no-intent bucket; Singer and Oktay identify ghost postings from features of the posting itself (Business Economics, 2025); the Congressional Research Service states plainly that no official statistics exist (In Focus IF12977, April 2025). None of it is connected to a wage. The main paper measures a wage consequence and cannot see intent. This draft asks what can be learned about the split from data that already exist, what the first pass through Kevin's own panels shows, and what the decisive version of the study requires.

One correction to the two-box framing before it hardens. There are three kinds of long-lived posting, not two. A genuine vacancy in a hard search. A phantom posting with no intent to hire. And a continuous-recruitment posting: real intent, no single vacancy, kept open because the employer hires the role whenever a qualified applicant appears. Hospitals and professional-services firms run these permanently. They convert, but slowly and on no deadline. Any design that sorts postings into genuine and phantom will misfile the third box, and the third box is concentrated in exactly the industries where unfilled share is highest. The design below treats "converts eventually, on no schedule" as its own predicted pattern rather than as evidence for either side.

## 2. Separating genuine from phantom without intent data

No public dataset records whether an employer meant to hire. The separation has to come from behavior: how long postings live, and whether they produce hires. Five measures, in the order a study would use them.

**2.1 Implied duration from JOLTS stocks and flows.** Publisher: BLS, Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey, state estimates and national industry estimates. The openings stock divided by the monthly hires flow gives the months the current stock would take to clear at the current hire rate, the steady-state mean duration of a posting under a constant fill hazard. Free and public, monthly for industries and, through December 2025, for states. Known failure modes: hires include people hired without any posting (walk-ins, referrals, internal transfers), so in fast-churn industries hires exceed openings and the implied duration falls below one month, which is a statement about the measure, not the market; the calculation assumes a steady state that 2021 and 2022 were not; the state estimates are model-based; and duration and unfilled share computed from the same two series are transforms of one ratio, so they cannot corroborate each other. What implied duration can do is set the level that any posting-level dataset has to match, and locate the cells where the stock is old.

**2.2 Conversion dynamics from JOLTS.** Publisher: BLS JOLTS, same series. Three statistics that are not transforms of the unfilled share: the three-month clearance rate (hires over the next three months per opening today); the pipeline elasticity (how much next quarter's hires rise when today's openings stock rises, holding today's hires constant); and the within-cell persistence of the openings stock. A genuine stock converts with a lag, so its pipeline elasticity is positive. A dead stock, postings that will never hire, shows a pipeline elasticity near zero no matter how large it grows. Free and public. Known failure modes: openings are endogenous, employers post more when they expect to hire, so a positive elasticity is necessary for the genuine reading but not sufficient proof of it; a phantom layer underneath a genuine stock does not turn the elasticity off, it only dilutes it, so these statistics bound the phantom share loosely rather than measuring it; and continuous-recruitment postings convert slowly by design, which mimics one phantom signature (low clearance) while failing the other (their pipeline elasticity stays positive because the hires do arrive).

**2.3 Posting-level duration distributions.** Publisher: Lightcast, Job Posting Analytics (posted and expired timestamps on each posting, a published posting-duration metric). This is the decisive dataset: the share of postings in a state or industry that expire unfilled, the duration distribution's right tail, reposting of identical roles. Availability: proprietary, commercial license, including academic licensing through institutions; the federal government buys it sole-source. Indeed's posting-level data has the same status; what Indeed gives away is aggregates. Known failure modes: a posting's removal is not a hire (postings expire, get refreshed, get reposted under a new requisition, and each looks like a death and a birth); Lightcast's deduplication is proprietary, and up to 80 percent of collected postings are deduplicated away, so the unit "one posting" is partly a modeling choice; coverage is online postings only, while JOLTS counts all openings, so the two universes differ by construction.

**2.4 Public posting aggregates and vendor ratios.** Publishers: Indeed Hiring Lab (postings index and new-postings index, daily, by sector and state, free via its data page and FRED, series IHLIDXUS and IHLIDXNEWUS); Greenhouse (published ghost-share classifications by industry, free as reports, microdata proprietary); Revelio Labs (hires per posting from public profiles, proprietary through WRDS and institutional licenses; its published headline is that hires per posting halved between 2019 and 2024, from 8 per 10 postings to 4 per 10). These give priors and cross-checks, not cell-level identification. Known failure modes: one job board is not the market; platform clienteles are selected (Greenhouse skews to firms that buy applicant-tracking software); profile-based hire detection lags and misses non-profiled workers.

**2.5 Disclosure-regime filings.** Publishers: Ontario's regime (in force January 1, 2026) and the disclosure bills moving in New Jersey, Kentucky and California; the CRS note above is the map. If disclosure rules force postings to state whether a real vacancy exists and whether it was filled, they manufacture the intent variable this literature has never had. Availability: public in principle, not yet a usable time series. This is a 2027 data source, named here so the design in section 4 has a forward path that does not depend on a vendor contract.

**The best public substitute, stated plainly.** Everything in 2.1 and 2.2, run on BLS data, plus the Indeed aggregates as a cross-check on posting volumes, plus the published vendor shares (roughly a fifth of postings, from two independent sources) as a prior for the size of the layer the public data cannot see. That substitute can kill the crude version of the phantom story (a stock that never converts). It cannot price the layer. The decisive object, posting-level duration by state and industry, is Lightcast or nothing, and that is a licensing decision, not a methods problem.

## 3. First pass: what Kevin's panels already show

Computed November 1, 2026 from the state panel (master_labor_panel_v2.csv) and the industry panel (industry_panel.csv). One warning before the numbers, because it disciplines all of them: within any single month, unfilled share and implied duration are the same ratio read in two directions (duration is openings over hires; unfilled share is one minus hires over openings). Finding that high-unfilled cells have long implied durations is arithmetic, not evidence. The evidence is in the dynamics: clearance, pipeline elasticity, persistence, and in how duration levels changed between the pre-COVID window and the recent one.

**A data-integrity flag that changes the industry evidence.** The industry panel's professional and business services series was pulled with JOLTS industry code 600000, which is the education and health services supersector; the panel's education and health series is that supersector's components (610000 and 620000) summed. The two series are the same industry: correlation 1.0000 over 135 months, never more than one thousand apart in level. Professional and business services (JOLTS code 540000) never enters the panel. The industry panel therefore has nine distinct industries, not ten, and every statement in the main paper that names professional and business services as a separate industry, including its unfilled share near 47 percent and its per-industry wage regression, is currently a second copy of education and health. The fix is one re-pull of the 540000 series. Until it is made, the industry results below report education and health once and treat professional and business as missing. This flag is stated here rather than footnoted because the main paper's industry chart and two of its paragraphs lean on the duplicated cell.

**Industries (recent window January 2022 onward; the national industry panel in the file runs to July 2026).** The pattern across the nine real industries is slower conversion after COVID, not stopped conversion.

| Industry | Unfilled share | Implied duration (months) | 3-month clearance | Pipeline elasticity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Education & health | 0.53 | 2.15 | 1.41 | +0.19 (p < 0.001) |
| Financial activities | 0.54 | 2.22 | 1.38 | +0.14 (p < 0.001) |
| Information | 0.37 | 1.74 | 1.82 | +0.15 (p = 0.07) |
| Manufacturing | 0.35 | 1.58 | 1.92 | +0.32 (p < 0.001) |
| Other services | 0.31 | 1.49 | 2.07 | +0.07 (p = 0.18) |
| Trade, transp., utilities | 0.11 | 1.15 | 2.66 | +0.24 (p < 0.001) |
| Leisure & hospitality | 0.10 | 1.14 | 2.64 | +0.23 (p < 0.001) |
| Construction | −0.14 | 0.92 | 3.42 | +0.14 (p < 0.001) |

Education and health, the highest-unfilled industry that is actually in the panel, lengthened after COVID: implied duration 1.74 months before COVID, 2.15 after; three-month clearance fell from 1.74 to 1.41 hires per opening. Its pipeline elasticity stayed clearly positive in both windows (0.26 before, 0.19 after). A stock that grows and then produces hires at a stable proportional rate is behaving like a hard search, or like continuous recruitment, not like a wall of dead postings. The one industry whose recent pipeline elasticity is not distinguishable from zero is other services (0.07, p = 0.18), a small industry whose unfilled share is mid-pack; if any industry cell deserves a phantom look on dynamics alone, it is that one, and it is not a cell the main paper's story runs through. Construction and leisure sit where the paper put them: hires at or above the openings stock, durations at or below one month, the fast-churn end.

**States, by the frozen terciles (test window January 2022 to December 2025).** The question the draft was commissioned to answer: is the high-friction tercile's unfilled share duration-backed, or is its stock the stale kind?

| Frozen tercile | Unfilled share | Implied duration (months) | 3-month clearance | Pipeline elasticity (within state) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 0.28 | 1.41 | 2.13 | +0.25 (p < 0.001) |
| Mid | 0.33 | 1.53 | 1.97 | +0.28 (p < 0.001) |
| High | 0.37 | 1.64 | 1.85 | +0.28 (p < 0.001) |

The high tercile's stock is older (duration 1.64 months against 1.41 in the low tercile) and clears more slowly, which is what its higher unfilled share says in other words. The discriminating number is the pipeline elasticity, estimated within state so state size cannot manufacture it: +0.28 in the high tercile, the same as the mid tercile and slightly above the low, and up from +0.18 in the same states before COVID. State by state, the pipeline elasticity is positive in 16 of the high tercile's 17 states (median +0.30). Within-state persistence of the openings stock is high everywhere (about 0.88 in the recent window, all three terciles alike), so persistence does not separate the terciles either. On published flows, the high-friction states' openings behave like the rest of the country's openings, only slower: they convert, at a normal proportional rate, into the next quarter's hires.

**The bridge to wages, attempted and not found.** If conversion behavior were the difference between the high tercile's states, the states whose wage response is strongest should be the states whose stock converts best or worst. They are not, in either direction. Across all 51 states, the correlation between a state's recent-window wage coefficient and its implied duration is +0.03; with clearance, −0.01. Inside the high tercile alone (17 states, so read this as absence of a pattern, not proof of none): duration −0.30, clearance +0.28, with New Jersey (wage coefficient +1.49, duration an unremarkable 1.51 months) sitting off any line the others draw. Aggregate conversion behavior does not sort the wage responses. Whatever distinguishes the states where friction priced into wages, it is not visible in how their openings convert on average.

**What the first pass supports, and what it cannot distinguish.** Supported: the crude phantom story is dead at both levels. The cells carrying the main paper's claims (high-friction states after 2022; education and health among industries) do not hold dead stock; their openings convert into hires over the following quarter at elasticities as large as anywhere else, and in the high tercile larger than the same states managed before COVID. Also supported: conversion genuinely slowed after 2022 in the high-unfilled cells (duration up roughly a quarter in education and health, clearance down), so the post-COVID stock is older, not just bigger. Not distinguishable: a phantom layer of the size the intent literature estimates, roughly a fifth of postings. Under a genuine stock of four-fifths, every statistic in this section looks exactly as it does. The first pass rules out "mostly phantom." It is silent on "a fifth phantom," and a fifth is large enough to matter for the scarcity reading if that fifth is concentrated in the high-friction states and grew after 2022. Only posting-level duration data can weigh the layer, by comparing measured posting lifetimes against the 1.6 to 2.2 month durations JOLTS implies, and by counting postings that expire with no hire at all.

## 4. The full study, if it goes forward

**Estimand.** Inside the main paper's frozen high tercile, on the January 2022 to December 2025 window, the difference in the friction-to-wage coefficient between phantom-leaning and genuine-leaning cells, where a cell is a state (or, with industry duration data, a state-by-industry cell) and the leaning is assigned by a phantom proxy fixed in advance: the share of the cell's postings still open at sixty days, or expiring with no recorded hire, from posting-level data. In regression form, the paper's preferred specification with friction interacted with the cell's phantom share, the coefficient on the interaction being the estimand: how much of the +0.650 lives where postings behave like phantoms.

**Design.** Four steps, registered before the duration data are pulled. (1) Build the phantom proxy by state and by industry from Lightcast posting lifetimes (posted to expired, deduplicated), aggregated over 2022 to 2025, and validate it against the public benchmarks: it should reproduce the JOLTS-implied duration ranking of industries and sit near the published vendor shares in level. (2) Split the frozen high tercile's cells at the proxy's median, fixed on 2015 to 2019 data where the proxy reaches back that far, and re-run the paper's wage specification with the split interacted. (3) Run the identical split on 2015 to 2019 as the placebo window; the main paper's design says no cell should show the effect there, phantom or genuine. (4) Report the education and health cell separately under both its correct identity and with the repaired professional and business series (JOLTS 540000) restored to the industry panel, so the industry chapter of the main paper is re-based on ten real industries.

**The falsification line, written down in advance.** If the wage effect is as strong in phantom-leaning cells as in genuine-leaning ones, the scarcity interpretation fails. The coefficient is then re-read as a postings-measurement artifact, the main paper's located effect narrows to a claim about posted data rather than hiring difficulty, and this follow-up reports that as its headline. The symmetric line also binds: if the effect concentrates in genuine-leaning cells and vanishes in phantom-leaning ones, the scarcity reading survives its hardest available test, and the phantom literature gains its first wage result, namely that the phantom margin itself is priced at zero.

**Data costs.** The public route costs nothing and buys sections 2.1, 2.2 and 2.4: BLS JOLTS and Indeed Hiring Lab aggregates, already in hand. The decisive route is a Lightcast license (institutional or academic; pricing is by quote and is not published, so no figure is stated here), or a research partnership for posting-level Indeed data, or WRDS access for Revelio. A zero-cost partial substitute worth one paragraph in the eventual paper: state workforce agencies in a few states publish their own job-bank posting durations, unstandardized and unverified here, usable as spot checks only. The honest budget line is that the study's cost is a data negotiation, measured in months of access work, not a compute problem.

## 5. Verdict: a section in the current paper now, short and bounded; the study stays a paper

Recommendation: add a short section to The Tightness Paradox now, reporting the dynamics check, and keep the full genuine-versus-phantom split as the standalone follow-up it already is on the research list. Three reasons.

First, the paper has already promised this reader question an answer path. Its limits section names a friction measure built from posting-level duration data as the first thing that would change its conclusions, and its closing claim ("the phantom is real, it concentrates where predicted") invites the obvious follow-up: are the postings behind the one positive result real? A paper whose method is reporting every test it ran should not leave the one test the public data already supports sitting in a drawer, especially when the test cuts in the paper's favor in the crude version and the paper's credibility comes from printing the cuts that did not.

Second, the first pass does real work that only this section can do. It kills the strongest cheap objection to the headline, that the high-friction states' coefficient is built on a dead stock of postings that never hire. On the published flows that objection is testable, and it fails: the stock converts, at a normal rate, in the very states and years the coefficient comes from. Printing that, next to the explicit statement that a fifth-sized phantom layer would be invisible to the same test, is the paper's house style: the result and its boundary in the same breath.

Third, the standalone study should not be rushed into the paper, because its decisive input does not exist in hand. The phantom split requires posting-level durations, a licensing negotiation, and a repaired industry panel. Folding a half-built version into the paper now would either overclaim (a split built on JOLTS dynamics alone would be the mechanical transform warning of section 3 wearing a costume) or stall the paper behind a data contract. The section now, the study when the data land.

Two conditions on the section, both cheap. It reports the tercile dynamics table and the education and health duration change, and it does not use the word phantom about professional and business services until the 540000 re-pull lands; and it carries the layer caveat in its own text, that aggregate flows rule out a mostly-phantom stock and are silent on a layer of the size the intent literature estimates. With those, the section strengthens the paper it joins. Without the second, it would claim more than the first pass found, which is the one thing this paper has never done.
